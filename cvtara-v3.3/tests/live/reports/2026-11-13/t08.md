# t08: Sceptical shopper from Google (logged out, then customers[16], free)

Screenshots: `tests/live/results/agents/t08/`. Run 2026-11-13, 04:24–04:36 UTC (the session stopped on a usage limit; written up at 07:50 UTC, past the deadline). Laptop, Chrome 1366x860.

## 1. Who I am and what I came to do
I searched Google for "data analyst resume Bangalore" and landed on a CVTara city/role page. I have been burned before by "free" resume builders that charge at the last step, so before signing up I check whether the site is trustworthy and whether its prices beat Canva, Naukri, Zety and Resume Worded.

## 2. First 10 seconds
- **What I understood:** "Data Analyst resume for jobs in Bangalore", free to build, pay once, no auto-renewal. The page is clean and looks professional, with a serif heading and a calm blue colour scheme. I stayed.
- **What I felt:** suspicion straight away, for three reasons:
  1. A loud orange "FESTIVE OFFER · Diwali Dhamaka 2026 · Claim this offer" bar. It is 13 Nov, Diwali has passed, and the bar never says what the offer is.
  2. The city page is thin. It has two example bullets, six skills and two interview questions. It reads like a template filled in per city.
  3. The page says "Made by an HR team in Bangalore", but the footer says "Built by an HR professional" (one person). Nobody is named anywhere.
- **Would I stay?** Yes, long enough to read the pricing. The "no auto-renewal" promise is the strongest hook on the page.

## 4. Problems, most serious first
1. **The Pro plan contradicts itself on the same card. Major (pricing honesty).** Home page `/#pricing`, `/checkout.html` and the `/pricing/` comparison table all say **30** tailored resumes and **30** cover letters. The bullet list on the same Pro card, and the Pro section of `/pricing/`, say **20 and 20**. To a sceptic this looks like bait. Screenshots: `home_pricing_cards.png`, `checkout.png`. Fix: use one number everywhere, ideally served from one config value.
2. **"Diwali Dhamaka 2026" offer bar with no offer. Major (trust and possibly consumer law).** The bar is on every public page, five days after Diwali. "Claim this offer" goes to `/register?next=/checkout.html`, and checkout then shows the full ₹299 with discount ₹0. Festive coupon guesses are rejected. A promotion that promises an offer and delivers none is a dark pattern, and Indian consumer protection rules on misleading advertising could apply. Screenshots: `offer_click.png`, `checkout.png`. Fix: remove the bar, or state the exact offer ("₹50 off Job Hunt till 15 Nov") and apply it automatically.
3. **The Contact page contradicts the refund policy. Major (trust).** `/contact.html`, under "Quick answers", links to "the No-Refund Policy", while `/refund.html` promises a 7-day refund. Screenshot: `contact.png`. Fix: rename the link to "Refund Policy".
4. **No identifiable business behind the site. Major (trust).** The site lists no registered company name (Pvt Ltd / LLP / proprietor), no GSTIN and no named founder. The founder quote is signed "Founder, CVTara". The address is a co-working park (IndiQube, Domlur). The privacy "grievance contact" is just the support inbox, with no named officer. Zety, Resume.io and Naukri (Info Edge) all name the legal entity. Fix: add "CVTara is operated by <legal name>, GSTIN…" in the footer, Terms and Privacy, and name the founder with a LinkedIn link.
5. **No reviews or social proof at all. Major (conversion).** The site has no ratings and no testimonials with names. Checkout offers an opt-in "recently started" notice, but I never saw one. Fix: add a few real, attributed reviews, or link a Google Business or Trustpilot profile.
6. **The 7-day refund is narrower than it sounds. Minor (honesty).** "Used" includes one clean download or one paid ATS scan. For Quick Download, the download is the whole product, so in practice the ₹99 plan is never refundable once you get what you paid for. Say this plainly on the pricing page.
7. **Products appear in the refund policy and at checkout but not on the pricing page. Minor.** "Tara Expert Review" (₹499) and "Profile Boost" (₹699) show up only in those two places. Tara Expert Review is described as "an instant written review… ready in minutes", yet the refund policy says "the CVTara team starts on it". It is unclear whether a human writes it.
8. **"Pricing" in the top navigation goes to `/#pricing`, not `/pricing/`. Minor.** From an SEO page it throws me back to the home page, while the footer links to `/pricing/`.
9. **The pricing hero has a "Use this template" button. Polish.** It is a copy-paste leftover; there is no template on the pricing page.
10. **Slow Contact page and intermittent backend errors. Minor.** `/contact.html` timed out once at 25 s on `domcontentloaded` (4.4 s on retry). `/api/csrf` twice answered a non-JSON "upstream r…" error, which looks like a proxy timeout. One workspace load fires about 35 API calls, with duplicates: `/api/allowance` 5 times, `/api/profile` 4 times and `/api/resumes` 4 times.
11. **The Tara chat opens by itself over the dashboard. Minor (UX).** Screenshot: `after_login.png`. The first-step card and the stats are half hidden. Fix: open it only on click, or as a small nudge.
12. **The price-fairness pop-up comes too early. Polish.** "Is CVTara's price fair for you?" appeared right after my first save, before I had looked at any price.
13. **The free watermark is not "light". Polish.** The preview is covered in diagonal "CVTara · Preview" text (`b2.png`). It is fair to watermark, but the pricing copy should not call it light.
14. **Inconsistent checkout total. Polish.** The checkout says "You pay ₹299" with no line saying whether GST is included.

## 6. Compared with competitors
- **Zety** (official US page: $1.95 for a 14-day trial, then auto-renews at $25.95 every 4 weeks; rupee prices unconfirmed): Zety has a large review base, a named company and a polished content writer. CVTara does better on honesty: there is no trial trap and no auto-renewal. Its biggest selling point is pricing and it under-sells it. A "Zety charges ₹2,000+/month after the trial; we charge ₹299 once" comparison would convert.
- **Resume Worded** (about $49/month, $99/quarter, $229/year): its resume scoring and LinkedIn review are deeper and backed by a known brand. CVTara's ATS Match with a gap report costs a tiny fraction of that and is India-specific (Naukri, notice period, CTC).
- **Canva** (free templates; Pro about ₹500/month): Canva is free to download with no watermark, which is the most dangerous competitor for a fresher who only needs one PDF. Its layouts are often not ATS-friendly, and it has no job matching or cover letters. CVTara needs to say why ₹99 beats free Canva: a text-based, ATS-safe PDF plus a gap report.
- **Naukri** (profile free; FastForward paid services such as Resume Display at about ₹1,395, unverified): recruiters actually search Naukri. CVTara cannot replace that, but it is cheaper than Naukri's paid resume services and can sit next to Naukri.

## 7. Would I pay?
Probably **Quick Download at ₹99** once, and only after the trust fixes. I would not pay ₹299 or ₹599 today, because the Pro card cannot agree with itself on 20 or 30 resumes, the offer bar promised something that was not there, and I cannot tell which company takes my money. To pay sooner I would need a named legal entity, real reviews, an offer that is either honest or absent, and one consistent set of plan numbers.

## 8. Is this the best site for me?
For an Indian job seeker who wants a clean, ATS-safe resume without a subscription trap, the product is probably the best value I compared. The builder is good and the pricing model is fair. The site does not yet *look* trustworthy enough to prove it, and a sceptic like me leaves before finding out.

## 9. Top 5 changes
1. Fix Pro to one number (20 or 30) on every page and at checkout.
2. Remove or honestly specify the Diwali offer, and auto-apply any discount it promises.
3. Add the legal entity name, GSTIN and a named founder, and change "No-Refund Policy" to "Refund Policy" on the Contact page.
4. Add real, attributed reviews and a short comparison box against Zety, Canva and Resume Worded on `/pricing/`.
5. Point the nav "Pricing" link to `/pricing/`, stop the Tara chat from opening by itself, and enrich city pages beyond two bullets.

## 11. What I did not like and who does it better
- **Trust signals:** Resume.io and Zety show the company name, review counts (Trustpilot) and named writers. CVTara should copy the entity disclosure and the attributed reviews.
- **Promotions:** Kickresume and Novoresume state the exact discount and the end date and apply it automatically. CVTara's offer bar should do the same or go.
- **Plan clarity:** Rezi and Enhancv each have one comparison table that matches their cards. CVTara should generate the cards, the table and checkout from one source.
- **Free tier versus Canva:** Canva gives a free download with no watermark. CVTara should beat it on substance: show a side-by-side "Canva PDF vs CVTara PDF read by an ATS" demo on the pricing page.
- **Content depth on SEO pages:** Naukri's and Indeed India's career guides carry more local detail (salary ranges, companies, real ads). City pages with two bullets will look thin to Google and to readers.
