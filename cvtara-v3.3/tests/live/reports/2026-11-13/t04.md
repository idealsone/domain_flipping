## 4. Problems (most serious first)
1. **Major: no senior roles in interview practice.** `/workspace.html?view=interview`, "Start a mock interview". All 24 roles are executive or associate level, and searching "Operations Manager" says "No match yet". Pricing sells Pro for "senior roles" and "interview practice", yet a manager cannot practise for his own job. (`11-role-search.png`) **Fix:** add role families such as Operations Manager/Head, General Manager, Plant Head, Project/Program Manager, Sales Head and HR Head, each with leadership and stakeholder questions. Let users type a free-text role and generate questions from it.
2. **Major (trust): Pro allowance is stated two different ways.**
   - On `/pricing/`, the compare table says "30 resumes + 30 cover letters", but the Pro card says "20 tailored resumes and 20 cover letters".
   - On the Billing page, the same Pro card shows big numbers "30 / 30 / 60" and then the bullet "20 tailored resumes and 20 cover letters". (`02-pricing.png`, `20-billing.png`)
   - The Job Hunt card on /pricing/ leaves out the 4 mock interviews that the table lists, and the Pro card does not say "12 mock interviews".

   Anyone paying ₹599 will notice. **Fix:** one source of truth for plan facts.
3. **Major (my persona): the templates gallery greets seniors with junior samples.** The default gallery is all 2022–2023 graduates. The senior samples appear only after the picker loads and you go through it. **Fix:** remember the experience level and load senior samples, or put a "Senior / Executive" collection up front.
4. **Major: internal library names are shown to customers.** The Layout dropdown on `/templates.html` lists "Rendercv-opal", "Latexcv-classic", "Rendercv-sb2nov", "Html-executive", "Latexcv-infographics2", "Career-sidebar-ceramic" and more. These are raw code slugs, they look amateur, and they show third-party template sources. **Fix:** give them human names or hide the filter.
5. **Major: misleading add-on name.** "Tara Expert Review – an instant written review … ready in minutes" for ₹499. "Expert" implies a human, but "instant" says it is automatic. A senior buyer will feel tricked. Also, "Profile Boost" at ₹699 costs more than the whole Pro plan, which looks odd. **Fix:** rename it "Instant resume review", or make it a real human review.
6. **Minor: the Tara chat auto-opens over the dashboard** and covers the stats and choices (`08-after-login.png`). Intercom-style opening on the first visit only would be fine.
7. **Minor: the "Your mock interview feedback is ready" toast** says **"Update status"** where it should say "Read feedback". It also stays on every page and covers the sidebar plan box (`16`, `19`, `20`).
8. **Minor: the dashboard is cluttered.** It shows three separate "what next" blocks: the next-step card, "What brings you…?" and "What would you like…?". On top of that it has an "Application workspace" step list. My profile was auto-set to "Data Analyst, fresher", which is wrong for me and was never asked.
9. **Minor: the interview flow feels thin for a manager.** A "standard" mock is 5 questions, two of them generic ("strengths", "why change"). The level shown was "Beginner" even though the first answer scored 4/4, 3/4, 4/4, 3/4, 3/4. That was fair here, because I pasted the same story into the other four answers, but the label stings.
10. **Speed: two page loads of `/workspace.html` timed out after 30 s** at about 04:35 UTC, while plain curl to `/` answered in 0.4 s. Home took 4.7 s and the templates page 4.9 s until load. That is slow for a "premium" feel.
11. **Polish:**

## 6. Compared with competitors
This comparison comes from publicly known pricing models. I did no fresh web lookups because of time, so treat the prices as approximate.
- **Zety:** offers a cheap trial that rolls into a recurring roughly monthly charge, many executive-looking templates, and a content library of pre-written bullets by job title, managers included. CVTara is much better on honesty: one payment, no auto-renew, and INR/UPI. Zety is better on pre-written senior bullet content.
- **Resume.io:** has a polished, premium-feeling editor, live preview and strong typography, also on a trial-then-recurring model. Its templates look more "C-suite" than CVTara's. CVTara is better on price, ATS match against a pasted job, and Indian context such as ₹ crore and Naukri.
- **Kickresume:** has more visually striking templates, executive examples written by real people, and freemium plus a subscription. CVTara is better on interview practice with written feedback and on no subscription. Kickresume is better on senior design range.
- **Big Interview / Yoodli-style tools:** have role libraries that include managers and executives. CVTara's interview roles currently stop at store manager.

## 10. Security
- **Admin API as a customer:** GET `/api/admin/overview`, `/admin/settings`, `/admin/purchases`, `/admin/letter-summary` all returned **403** "You do not have access". Blocked as it should be.
- **Script/HTML injection:**
  - In the interview answer I typed `<script>alert(1)</script><b>bold</b>`. It showed as plain text in the feedback, with no dialog and no bold (`19-feedback.png`). Safe. The feedback even told me to "remove stray formatting characters".
  - In the Company field I typed `<img src=x onerror=alert(1)>`. No dialog fired, and no `img[src=x]` element exists in the page. Safe.
- **Other users' data by `owner_id` parameter** (QA tester 13's id, 29): `/api/profile`, `/resumes`, `/purchases`, `/allowance` and `/tara/conversations` with `?owner_id=29`. By the time I ran this my session had expired, so every call answered **401 "Sign in to continue"**. That proves unauthenticated access is refused, but the signed-in test is **not done**. The signed-in API calls I made returned only my own data (my `owner_id` 30). The ids are sequential small integers, so this test is worth re-running while signed in.
- **Information leak (minor):** GET `/api/purchases` returns internal fields to the customer: `provider_order_id`, `payment_reference: "manual_qa14"` and `is_live`. That is harmless, but trim it to what the customer needs.
- **Not attempted:**
  - Price tampering at checkout: opening checkout was blocked by my own safety tooling, so no request was captured.
  - Coupon and referral reuse, and non-resume upload: out of time.

## 11. What I did not like and who does it better
- **Senior templates.** Kickresume and Resume.io show executive examples up front, with distinctive typography. CVTara's senior designs are correct but generic, and are hidden behind a picker. Copy their "Executive" collection page that shows senior examples.
- **Pre-written senior content.** Zety suggests manager-level bullets by job title. CVTara's builder should offer Ops Head bullets such as P&L, multi-site, vendor and cost-out.
- **Interview roles.** Big Interview has management and leadership tracks. CVTara stops at store manager.
- **Plan clarity.** Novoresume and Enhancv show one plan card with numbers that never contradict. Copy that discipline.
- **Brand tone.** "Diwali Dhamaka" banners suit Naukri or Flipkart promotions. Premium tools like Resume.io keep a calm, consistent brand. Remove festive banners after the festival, or make them subtle.
- **What CVTara does better than all of them:**
  - No subscription trap.
  - INR and UPI.
  - The ATS-against-a-real-job idea.
  - Interview feedback that is genuinely specific and honest: it noticed I reused the same answer.

  Keep these and lead with them.
