What a resume score means

What does a resume score mean? A resume match score measures how many keywords from a job description appear in your resume and how strong your action verbs are. It does not read your meaning or predict whether you will be hired.

What the score measures

  • Keyword overlap — words in the job description that also appear in your resume.
  • Verb strength — action verbs (built, delivered, reduced) score higher than passive phrases (was responsible for, helped with).
  • Coverage — whether required skills in the JD appear somewhere in your resume.

What the score cannot see

  • Whether your claims are true
  • Whether you're right for the culture
  • Salary expectation fit
  • Years-of-experience requirements that live outside the text

A higher score increases your chance of passing a keyword filter. A recruiter then decides.

How to improve your score

Add naturally-occurring keywords from the JD that your resume genuinely covers. Swap passive phrases for strong verbs. Do not stuff — a recruiter will read the result.
